# Export controls and geopolitics

Trade and national-security policy is a recurring constraint on NVIDIA's business (10-K FY2022).

## FY2022 baseline (10-K FY2022)

- **Russia**: after the February 2022 invasion of Ukraine, US/allied sanctions and export controls led NVIDIA to **cease product sales to Russia**; potential loss of assets in Russia/Ukraine regions.
- **Entity List / restricted parties**: restrictions on doing business without an export license; deemed-export rules could limit R&D execution (foreign-national employees).
- **Manufacturing concentration**: wafer fab and final assembly concentrated in **Taiwan, China, Hong Kong, Israel, Korea** — geopolitical tension there could disrupt supply, demand, and market access.
- **China consumer regulation**: restrictions on gaming duration and game access may hurt the Gaming business.
- **AI-specific risk flagged**: increasing focus on the strategic importance of AI may bring restrictions targeting products that enable AI — limiting NVIDIA's ability to serve demand abroad.
- General exposure: 84% of FY2022 revenue billed outside the US; Israel operations subject to boycott risk.

## Timeline across the filings

**FY2023 (10-K FY2023)** — Q3 FY2023 (Aug/Sep 2022): US licensing requirements for **A100, H100, DGX systems and A100X** to China (incl. Hong Kong/Macau) and Russia; NVIDIA offered the **A800** as a China alternative and transitioned certain operations out of China.

**FY2024 (10-K FY2024)** — escalating rules:
- Jul 2023: additional licensing for a subset of A100/H100 to certain customers/regions incl. parts of the Middle East.
- **Oct 2023:** new/updated rules effective Q4 FY2024 for China and Country Groups **D1, D4, D5** (incl. Saudi Arabia, UAE, Vietnam; excluding Israel) covering products above performance thresholds — **A100, A800, H100, H800, L4, L40, L40S and RTX 4090** — effective immediately Oct 23, 2023 for A100/A800/H100/H800/L40S.
- **No licenses granted** for restricted China shipments; the **NAC** (Notified Advanced Computing) process yielded no approvals; alternatives shipped "in small volumes." China fell to 14% of Data Center revenue. NVIDIA says its competitive position "has been harmed."

**FY2025 (10-K FY2025)** — the AI Diffusion rule:
- Defines the controls as "a series of shifting and expanding export control restrictions."
- **Jan 15, 2025 "AI Diffusion" IFR:** would impose a **worldwide licensing requirement** on ECCN 3A090.a / 4A090.a products and systems containing them (DGX, HGX, MGX), covering **A100, A800, H100, H200, H800, B100, B200, GB200, GB200 NVL72/NVL36, L4, L40S, RTX 6000 Ada**. It divides the world into three tiers (150+ countries relegated to "Tier 2") with UVEU/NVEU benefits. NVIDIA quantifies the harms (reduced market, favored approved firms, foreign alternatives, retaliation/tariffs).
- China Data Center revenue grew on China-specific products but stayed well below pre-Oct-2023 levels. EU AI Act effective Aug 1, 2024; China investigating whether US-export-control compliance discriminates against Chinese customers.

**FY2026 (10-K FY2026)** — foreclosure and tariffs:
- **April 2025: H20 license requirement** for China (incl. HK/Macau) and D:5 → **$4.5B charge** in Q1 FY2026 for H20 excess inventory and purchase obligations.
- **August 2025:** licenses granted for certain H20 sales to certain China customers; ~**$60M** H20 revenue; USG officials "expressed an expectation" of **15% of licensed revenue** (not codified).
- **February 2026: H200 license** for small volumes to specific China customers — no revenue yet; US inspection required and a **25% import tariff** applies.
- **AI Diffusion IFR rescinded May 2025** with a replacement rule promised but uncertain; Senate passed the **GAIN AI Act** in the Oct 2025 NDAA.
- **"As of the end of fiscal year 2026, we were effectively foreclosed from competing in China's data center computing/compute market"** — and the foreclosure "helped our competitors build larger developer and customer ecosystems to challenge us worldwide."
- **China antitrust counter-pressure:** Sept 15, 2025 preliminary finding by China's regulators that NVIDIA's export-control-driven degraded products violated the terms of China's **Mellanox** approval (see [Mellanox](../entities/mellanox.md)); a Chinese government **Action Plan** endorses compute-performance-per-watt and memory-bandwidth standards.
- Russia: direct sales stopped and operations closed in FY2023 (all filings). Supply dependence on Taiwan/South Korea remains a core risk.

## Reading across filings

- **Pattern:** controls have expanded monotonically in scope and product coverage; each time NVIDIA designed a compliant product for China (A800 → H800 → H20 → H200), the rules moved again, and by FY2026 NVIDIA stopped offering a broadly competitive China data-center product.
- **Financial materiality grew:** from qualitative risk in FY2022 to a discrete **$4.5B charge** in FY2026, plus lost revenue and a materially changed geographic revenue mix (recast to 31% outside the US in FY2026).
- Controls name **each GPU generation**, tying policy directly to the [Blackwell and Rubin roadmap](blackwell-and-rubin.md).
