# Arm acquisition attempt

NVIDIA signed a Share Purchase Agreement with **SoftBank Group Corp.** in **September 2020** (with a prepayment at signing) to acquire **Arm Limited**, the UK-based CPU IP licensor.

## Termination (10-K FY2022)

- **February 8, 2022**: NVIDIA and SoftBank announced termination of the agreement because of **significant regulatory challenges** preventing completion.
- NVIDIA stated it would record a **$1.36 billion charge in Q1 FY2023** in operating expenses, writing off the prepayment made at signing.
- Strategic context: the deal would have extended NVIDIA's IP-licensing leverage (one of its five stated business strategies) and paired Arm CPUs with NVIDIA's accelerated-computing stack (Arm architecture underpins NVIDIA's later **Grace** CPU and automotive SoCs).
- Fallout flagged in risk factors: perceived difficulty consummating acquisitions may make future deals harder, more complex, or more expensive.

## Subsequent filings

- **FY2023 (10-K FY2023):** the charge was recorded at **$1.353B** in operating expenses ("acquisition termination cost"), vs the $1.36B estimated in the FY2022 10-K — a small refinement, not a restatement.
- **FY2024–FY2026:** Arm appears only as a prior-year comparative ($1.353B in FY2023) and in MD&A discussing acquisition-integration risk. NVIDIA's own Arm-based **Grace** CPU and automotive SoCs carried the Arm-architecture strategy forward organically.
- Contrast with FY2026's [Groq](../entities/groq.md) deal: rather than acquire a company, NVIDIA paid ~$17B to **license** technology and hire employees — an acquisition-alternative structure.
