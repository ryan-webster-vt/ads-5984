# Antitrust and competition scrutiny

As NVIDIA's AI position grew, competition regulators worldwide began requesting information about its GPU sales, supply allocation and ecosystem investments. This theme is mentioned across many pages in the wiki but had no page before this lint.

## Timeline across the filings

- **FY2024 (10-K FY2024):** "Our position in markets relating to AI has led to increased interest in our business from regulators worldwide." The **French Competition Authority** collected information about NVIDIA's business and competition in the graphics-card and cloud-service-provider market; NVIDIA also received requests from the **EU, UK and China** regarding GPU sales, supply allocation, foundation models, and investments/partnerships with foundation-model companies.
- **FY2025 (10-K FY2025):** broadened — "we have received, and continue to receive, broad requests for information from competition regulators in the **European Union, the United States, the United Kingdom, China, and South Korea**." NVIDIA frames this as an ongoing, expanding inquiry rather than a concluded case.
- **FY2026 (10-K FY2026):** the same list of jurisdictions plus the **China antitrust finding**: on **Sept 15, 2025**, China's antitrust regulators issued a **preliminary finding** that NVIDIA's compliance with US export controls (offering degraded products to the Chinese market) discriminated unfairly against Chinese customers and therefore violated the terms of China's approval of the [Mellanox](../entities/mellanox.md) acquisition. This is the one concrete adverse antitrust finding disclosed in the filings.

## Observations

- No **U.S. Department of Justice / FTC enforcement action is named** in any of these filings; the U.S. is described generically as one of the competition regulators requesting information.
- The scrutiny is tightly coupled to [export controls](export-controls.md): China's theory of harm is that U.S. export compliance is itself anticompetitive toward Chinese customers.
- [Risk factors](risk-factors.md) disclose the inquiries but record **no accrual**, because liabilities are not considered probable.
