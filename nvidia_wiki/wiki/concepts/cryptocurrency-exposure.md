# Cryptocurrency exposure

GPUs designed for [Gaming](gaming.md) can be used for digital-currency mining (e.g., Ethereum), and NVIDIA repeatedly states it **cannot estimate** mining's impact on demand with any precision — making demand forecasting hard (10-K FY2022).

## FY2022 facts (10-K FY2022)

- **CMP (Cryptocurrency Mining Processor)** revenue was **$550M** in FY2022 (nominal prior year), driving OEM & Other revenue +84%. CMP sales declined significantly in Q4 FY2022.
- From Q2 FY2022, nearly all desktop Ampere GeForce GPUs shipped as **LHR (Lite Hash Rate)** cards with limited Ethereum mining capability, to direct GeForce to gamers.
- Risk highlighted: the pending **Ethereum 2.0** standard (proof of stake) could decrease GPU mining, dump used cards on the aftermarket, depress retail prices, increase channel returns, and cut new-GPU demand. Successful hash-rate hacks of LHR cards could re-divert GeForce supply to miners.
- Crypto-mining demand was listed among drivers of FY2022 Gaming growth (alongside RTX 30 Series ramp, esports, work-from-home).

## FY2024–FY2026: no longer a revenue driver

- Crypto is no longer broken out as a revenue factor. By FY2024–FY2026 the filings retain crypto-mining (Ethereum 2.0 merge, gray market, demand-forecasting difficulty) only as a **risk factor and demand-estimation warning**, while Data Center AI dwarfs it (10-K FY2024, FY2025, FY2026).
- The FY2026 10-K still lists cryptocurrency as an example of GPUs used for "new, mercurial, or trendy applications," and notes Ethereum 2.0's 2022 merge reduced mining demand.

## Litigation echo

The securities class action *In re NVIDIA Corporation Securities Litigation* (N.D. Cal., filed Dec 2018) alleges false/misleading statements about **channel inventory and crypto-mining impact on GPU demand** in 2017–2018. Timeline across the filings:

- Dismissed March 2021; plaintiffs appealed.
- Ninth Circuit (Aug 25, 2023) **affirmed in part, reversed in part** the dismissal; rehearing en banc denied Nov 15, 2023 (10-K FY2024).
- Supreme Court **granted certiorari June 17, 2024**, then **dismissed the writ as improvidently granted Dec 11, 2024**; the Ninth Circuit judgment took effect **Feb 20, 2025** and the case was **remanded to the district court** (10-K FY2025, FY2026). No settlement amount disclosed; no accrual recorded (liability not probable).
- Related derivative suits (California and Delaware, incl. *Lipchitz*, *Nelson*, *Horanic*) remain stayed. See [Risk factors](risk-factors.md).
