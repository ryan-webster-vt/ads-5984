# Risk factors — cross-filing tracker

Hub page summarizing the principal risk themes each 10-K discloses, with links to dedicated pages where a theme warrants one.

## FY2022 10-K (filed March 2022)

**Industry & competition**
- Failure to meet evolving market needs across the four platforms; design-win risk.
- Competitors: **AMD and Intel** in discrete/integrated GPUs and accelerated computing; internal chip programs at **Alibaba, Alphabet, Amazon, Tesla**; SoC suppliers (Ambarella, Broadcom, Qualcomm, Renesas, Samsung); interconnect/switch suppliers (Arista, Cisco, Juniper, Marvell, Broadcom…). Some competitors own fabs and have greater resources; networking customers may also be competitors.

**Demand, supply, manufacturing**
- Demand-forecast difficulty from [cryptocurrency mining](cryptocurrency-exposure.md) (Ethereum 2.0), COVID behavior shifts, channel dynamics, gray market.
- 12+ month lead times; non-cancellable orders; excess-inventory risk (see [Supply chain](supply-chain.md)).
- Third-party manufacturing dependence (limited number of global suppliers; yields; priority given to competitors).
- Product defects/security vulnerabilities could force recalls and damage brand.

**Global operations**
- 84% of revenue outside the US; economic/political conditions; inflation raising costs.
- Israel operations subject to boycott risk; Russia-Ukraine war impact.
- Climate change: extreme weather (California HQ wildfire/power-shutoff exposure), energy/water availability, climate regulation costs, 100% renewable-energy goal by end of FY2025.
- Acquisition risk — exemplified by the [Arm termination](arm-acquisition.md).
- **Cybersecurity incident**: on Feb 23, 2022 NVIDIA learned of unauthorized network access by criminal threat actors who misappropriated proprietary information **including source code** and leaked some of it; credentials rotated, network hardened, law enforcement notified.
- Business disruptions (natural disasters, California earthquake/wildfire concentration, Asia operations).
- Customer concentration within the partner network; purchase-order-based sales cancellable at any time.
- Talent attraction/retention; dependence on longstanding executive team; succession planning.
- ERP implementation starting Q1 FY2023 (risk of reporting disruption).
- COVID-19 pandemic effects on workforce, demand sustainability, and supply.
- Operating-results fluctuation; stock-price volatility.

**Regulatory, legal, stock**
- Complex laws: IP, taxes, import/export, anti-corruption, data privacy, competition, AI, climate, cryptocurrency. **[Export controls](export-controls.md)**: Entity List, deemed exports, Russia sanctions; geopolitical concentration in Taiwan/China/HK/Israel/Korea; China gaming restrictions.
- **Responsible use of AI**: ethical concerns could slow adoption or cause reputational/legal harm.
- ESG scrutiny from shareholders.
- IP protection costs; foreign law weaker protection.
- **Data privacy**: GDPR/UK GDPR (fines up to €20M or 4% of worldwide revenue), CCPA/CPRA (from 2023), Virginia CDPA, Colorado Privacy Act (both 2023), data-localization and cross-border transfer rules.
- **Taxes**: IRS examination of FY2018–2019; audits in Germany, Israel, India; OECD/BEPS changes; **TCJA R&D capitalization from FY2023** (5-year domestic / 15-year foreign amortization) materially raising cash taxes.
- Litigation: securities class action on appeal ([crypto/channel inventory](cryptocurrency-exposure.md)); derivative suits stayed.
- **Indebtedness**: $11B in notes due by 2060; refinancing and covenant risk.
- Change in control: Delaware anti-takeover provisions; **Microsoft's 2000 Xbox agreement** gives Microsoft first/last rights of refusal on any offer for ≥30% of NVIDIA stock.

## FY2024 10-K (filed February 2024)

Same risk taxonomy, but the **AI buildout and geopolitics dominate**. New/expanded:
- **Demand concentration & AI durability:** Data Center demand surged on generative AI, but "the long-term trajectory is unknown"; demand expected to remain concentrated; NVIDIA had demand visibility for new products ramping later in FY2025.
- **Export controls** greatly expanded: Oct 2023 D1/D4/D5 rules, no China licenses, NAC process, "effectively excluding us from all or part of the China market" ([export controls](export-controls.md)).
- **Regulatory/[antitrust scrutiny](antitrust-scrutiny.md) of the AI position:** French Competition Authority inquiry; requests from EU, UK, China. Increased interest because of NVIDIA's market position.
- **Israel/Hamas conflict:** explicit operating risk with ~3,700 employees and military call-ups.
- **Energy and water for data centers:** customers' ability to expand capacity depends on "clean water and predictable energy."
- **AI regulation:** EU AI Act; Biden Executive Order; potential safety-test notifications.
- **Climate** as a dedicated risk (California wildfire power shutoffs, regulation costs); **cybersecurity** now a full Item 1C disclosure; **responsible use of AI** retained.
- Crypto/channel-inventory demand risk and **gray market** retained.

## FY2025 10-K (filed February 2025)

- **AI Diffusion IFR (Jan 2025)** becomes a marquee risk: worldwide licensing, three tiers, 150+ "Tier 2" countries, UVEU/NVEU, retaliation/tariffs, supply/distribution compliance burdens.
- **Broadened [antitrust scrutiny](antitrust-scrutiny.md):** French Competition Authority plus EU, US, UK, China, South Korea; explicitly about GPU sales, supply allocation, foundation models and ecosystem investments.
- **Customer concentration sharpened:** three direct customers at 12%/11%/11% plus an indirect customer ≥10%.
- **Supply/prepayment risk escalation:** "the risk of these impacts has increased and may continue to increase as our purchase obligations and prepaids have grown."
- **Tariffs** called out repeatedly; **AI regulation** (EU AI Act, EU Code of Practice); crypto/gray-market retained; climate retained incl. "high absolute energy requirements of our GPUs."
- **Stock split** and the **ten-for-one** restatement are not risk items but affect comparability.

## FY2026 10-K (filed February 2026)

- **New standalone risk: commercial arrangements and counterparty risk** — long-term capacity purchase obligations, financial guarantees (land/power/shell), and financing arrangements NVIDIA has been asked to offer ("We have not entered into any financing arrangements").
- **Groq IP-license risk:** incorporating licensed technology "may not occur on expected timelines or at all."
- **OpenAI investment/partnership risk:** no assurance the deal will be completed ([OpenAI](../entities/openai.md)).
- **China:** "effectively foreclosed" from the China data center market, benefiting competitors; **H20/H200 licensing and 25% tariff**; GAIN AI Act; Chinese antitrust finding on the Mellanox commitments; Chinese Action Plan standards; open-source models (DeepSeek, Qwen, KIMMI) as competitive/policy risk.
- **Data-center/energy/capital availability** as a demand constraint; **government chip-tracking/throttling mandates**.
- **Tax:** OECD Pillar Two, the "side-by-side" framework and the **OBBBA** (July 2025); IRS examining FY2023–FY2024.
- **Customer concentration intensifies:** one direct customer 22% and another 14% of revenue; three customers 25%/18%/13% of receivables.
- Through-line: over five filings the center of gravity moved from **crypto/COVID supply risk (FY2022)** to **AI demand concentration and geopolitics (FY2024–FY2026)** — while the demand-estimation/inventory risk warned about every year actually materialized in FY2023 (Ampere), FY2025 (Blackwell yields) and FY2026 (H20).
